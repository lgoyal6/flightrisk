lgoyal6 / flightrisk

flightrisk.

Which banks lose a meaningful part of their deposits next quarter, ranked from FDIC Call Report data covering every US bank from 2015 to 2026. The intuition everyone starts with is that falling deposits predict falling deposits.

Risk is U-shaped, and the right arm is the surprising one. A bank that just took in a large inflow is about as likely to have a drawdown next quarter as one that just bled. Lumpy money is transient money.

Why growth predicts a fall

A large inflow is usually one depositor parking a balance that will move again: a municipality, a title company, a corporate treasury after a raise. It arrives as growth and leaves as a drawdown. The read-across is direct and uncomfortable: an account that just received a funding round is a drawdown risk, not a safe one.

It is not a small-bank artifact. Both ends stay elevated in every size band, though the right arm flattens as banks get bigger, where a single depositor is a smaller share of the book.

The shuffled control is the number to read first. Train the same model on labels that have been shuffled and the top 1% comes back at 0.97x, with a within-quarter AUC of 0.503. The pipeline finds nothing when there is nothing to find, which is the claim every other number on this page depends on.

Ranking by last quarter's decline is worse than random, and still useful. Its within-quarter AUC is 0.406, below a coin flip, while its top 1% carries 3.49x lift. Both are correct: the U inverts the relationship across the middle of the distribution, so a rule that orders the whole list badly can still be right about the extreme.

Where it loses

Published because a model that only reports where its author wins is an advertisement.

  • The linear model did not survive the holdout. It scored 5.82x in the walk-forward backtest and 1.59x out of time, below naive persistence. Gradient boosting held at 5.76x. One of these numbers would have been the headline if the holdout had not been run. reports/out_of_time.csv
  • The macro features add nothing. Adding regime variables moves lift from 9.37x to 9.41x, inside the fold-to-fold spread. A macro-only baseline cannot rank inside a quarter at all, because it is constant within one. reports/backtest_models.csv
  • Pooled AUC is inflated and is not shown. The backtest writes those columns with INFLATED in the name. A model that only knows which quarter it is scores well pooled while being unable to rank inside any single quarter, which is the only ranking an alert list needs. reports/backtest_models.csv
  • Bank level is not account level. The question worth answering is which customer pulls their balance. This is the public analog of it: quarterly, not daily, and one row per bank rather than per depositor. README.md
  • The largest banks are the thinnest slice. Above $100B there are 1,198 bank-quarters against 104,675 under $300M, so the flattening right arm in that band rests on the least data. reports/base_rate_by_size.csv
  • The alert list here is anonymised. The scores and the model's own reasons are real, but a public ranking of named banks by deposit-flight risk reads as a claim about those banks rather than a demonstration of a method. reports/alerts_2026Q1.csv
